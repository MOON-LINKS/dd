Pattern found: standard Arabic business verticals (salon booking, garages, nurseries, event halls, school buses, tutoring centers, car dealers, maintenance firms) already have several local tools each. The best gaps are community and governance niches where the buyer is a volunteer or a manager of many units, like buildings.

## 1. Shared-building manager

**What:** WhatsApp-first tool for a building chairman or property manager: monthly dues per unit (recorded, not processed), shared expenses (elevator, cleaner, water, generator), receipts, reminders, a transparent balance page for residents, maintenance requests. No online payments at first.

- **Green:** universal pain; legal backing (Egypt Law 119/2008 for buildings of 5+ units; Lebanon Decree-Law 88/1983 requires an owners' association for 3+ units); referral built in (every resident sees it); best buyer is a property or maintenance manager with many buildings.
- **Red:** chairmen are unpaid volunteers with no budget; WhatsApp group plus notebook is good enough; Egyptian competitors exist (MulakHub with free signup, Mogtama3y); building disputes over money are political; low value per building.
- **Yellow (estimates):** about $4 per building per month. 60 / 600 / 3,000 buildings gives roughly $240 / $2,400 / $12,000 a month. About 250 buildings for $1,000 a month. Property-manager plan ($40 to $100 per month for 30+ buildings) is the better unit economics.
- **Blue:** PIVOT the buyer to property and maintenance managers, then test.
- **Test (2 to 3 weeks):** interview 10 building chairmen and 5 property or maintenance managers in Aley; show a mock balance page; check competitor traction.
- **Kill if:** fewer than 3 managers would pay, or most chairmen refuse to pay anything.

## 2. TUK TIK

**What:** subscription app for Lebanese tuk-tuk drivers and municipalities: driver directory, status (busy / available), reminders, ride price log with fixed zone pricing, fuel cost estimate. Start in Aley.

- **Green:** displaced workforce; a driver was already waiting for a tuk-tuk app; municipalities want registration; delivery use is safer.
- **Red:** passenger use is in a legal grey zone (2025 ministry enforcement; the ministry says tuk-tuks are registered as motorcycles). The user reports the action was reversed; I found no report confirming it (unverified). Competitor: Nehna Haddak app. Drivers have little cash; municipalities are poor and slow.
- **Yellow (estimates):** $3 to $5 per driver per month; about 250 paying drivers for $1,000 a month; municipal revenue small.
- **Blue:** PIVOT to a municipal registry (ID, vehicle, fare zones, public directory), free for drivers at first.
- **Test:** interview 15 to 20 Aley drivers; meet the Aley municipality; confirm the legal status locally.
- **Kill if:** fewer than 10 drivers commit and the municipality shows no interest.

## 3. Driving-school manager

**What:** scheduling for driving schools: students, instructors, cars, lesson slots, no-show reminders by WhatsApp, exam-date tracking, vehicle service dates, fee records (no payment processing).

- **Green:** recurring scheduling pain (instructor, car, and student must match); every target country has driving schools; only two Arabic competitors found (DriveManager, Motakid); non-financial.
- **Red:** small market (hundreds of schools per country, unverified); owners are low-tech and use notebooks and WhatsApp; one-by-one sales; competitors already offer booking, fleet, and online payment.
- **Yellow (estimates):** about $12 per school per month; 30 / 200 / 800 schools gives roughly $360 / $2,400 / $9,600 a month; about 85 schools for $1,000 a month.
- **Blue:** TEST, not build. Ceiling is low as a standalone company.
- **Test:** interview 10 school owners; ask what breaks (double-booking, no-shows, exam dates) and what they would pay.
- **Kill if:** fewer than 4 of 10 would pay anything.

## 4. BeauTek (salon booking)

**What:** booking system for beauty centers (services, hours, client booking), from $10 per month.

- **Green:** price gap against foreign tools (Vagaro from $25 a month; Fresha no longer subscription-free); no-commission pitch.
- **Red:** at least five alternatives active in Lebanon (Hjezle with Arabic and local payment methods, Barakat Booking, Zaffe, TheBooker, Fresha); features are baseline; Square Appointments has a free plan globally.
- **Yellow (estimates):** $10 per salon per month; 40 / 300 / 1,000 salons gives roughly $400 / $3,000 / $10,000; about 100 salons for $1,000 a month.
- **Blue:** KILL as a generic system. Revisit only if salon-owner interviews show a gap (for example WhatsApp-first for small independent salons outside Beirut).
